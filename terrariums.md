## The Essence
**Eyebrow:** THE ESSENCE

# Understanding the ecosystem.

A terrarium is a planted arrangement held within glass. Water evaporates from the substrate and leaves, collects as condensation on the cooler glass, and returns to the growing medium, where the roots absorb it.

In a closed terrarium this cycle largely takes place within the glass, so very little water needs to be added. An open terrarium exchanges air with the room and loses moisture more quickly, so it needs more regular attention. In both cases the plants are alive and need appropriate care.

## Core Maintenance
**Eyebrow:** CORE MAINTENANCE

# The three metrics of balance.

Light, water and airflow work together to keep a terrarium balanced. Closed and open terrariums manage each of them differently, so the guidance below is divided accordingly.

### Light
Keep your terrarium in bright, indirect natural light. Direct sunlight can heat the glass and damage the plants.

- **Closed terrariums:** Place away from windows that receive prolonged direct sunlight.
- **Open terrariums:** Provide bright, indirect light in a space with gentle natural airflow.

### Water
Water sparingly. The aim is light, even moisture, without leaving the substrate saturated.

- **Closed terrariums:** Mist lightly only when condensation is consistently absent or the substrate begins to look dry. Do not water simply because a number of days have passed.
- **Open terrariums:** Check the moss and growing medium regularly. A light mist every 2–3 days may be needed, depending on room conditions.

### Airflow
Airflow affects how much moisture stays inside the terrarium.

- **Closed terrariums:** A light mist on the glass is normal. If heavy condensation remains for more than 24 hours, open the lid for 2–3 hours to release excess moisture.
- **Open terrariums:** No manual ventilation is needed. Normal room airflow is enough.

## Substrate Stratification
**Eyebrow:** THE FOUNDATION

# Understanding the substrate layers.

Sylva Auris terrariums are built in layers. Together they support healthy root growth and give excess water somewhere to collect. The layers are listed here from top to bottom.

### 01 — Canopy
**Moss and plants**

The living surface of the composition. Plants add form and foliage, while moss covers the substrate and helps retain surface moisture.

### 02 — Growing Medium
**Peat moss, coco chips and perlite**

The main growing layer. Peat moss retains moisture, coco chips add structure, and perlite creates air spaces that allow the roots to breathe.

### 03 — Filtration Layer
**Activated charcoal**

A thin layer of activated charcoal sits beneath the growing medium. It helps keep the substrate environment fresh.

### 04 — Drainage Bed
**Gravel and pebbles**

The bottom layer, where excess water collects beneath the growing medium. This reduces the risk of waterlogged roots, but it does not replace careful watering.

## Precautions
**Eyebrow:** PRECAUTIONS

# Things to Avoid.

A few simple precautions will help keep your terrarium healthy over time.

### Overwatering
Too much water can saturate the substrate, damage the roots and cause persistent condensation. Add water in small amounts and check the terrarium before adding more.

### Direct sunlight
Direct sunlight heats the glass and can damage the plants. Choose a position with bright, indirect natural light.

### Opening closed terrariums unnecessarily
Frequent opening disturbs the moisture balance. Open a closed terrarium only to release heavy condensation or to carry out maintenance.

### Chemical fertilisers
The growing medium is prepared to support the plants. Additional fertiliser is generally unnecessary and can cause excessive growth or upset the balance of the arrangement.

### Heaters and air conditioners
Strong heat and dry airflow stress the plants and change moisture levels. Choose a stable indoor position away from direct heating or cooling.

## Common Questions
**Eyebrow:** COMMON QUESTIONS

# Frequently Asked Questions.

Answers to common questions about caring for your Sylva Auris terrarium.

### How often should I water my terrarium?
There is no single schedule for every terrarium. Closed terrariums need very little additional water. Mist lightly when condensation is consistently absent or the substrate begins to look dry. Open terrariums lose moisture more readily and may need light misting every 2–3 days, depending on their surroundings.

### Where should I place my terrarium?
Choose a stable indoor position with bright, indirect natural light. Keep it away from prolonged direct sunlight, heaters and air-conditioner vents. Avoid places where it could be knocked or exposed to sudden temperature changes.

### Why is there condensation inside the glass?
Condensation is part of the moisture cycle. Water evaporates from the substrate and plants, collects on the cooler glass and returns to the growing medium. A light mist on the glass is normal. If heavy condensation remains for more than 24 hours, open the lid for 2–3 hours.

### Can I open my terrarium?
A closed terrarium does not need to be opened routinely. Open it when heavy condensation persists, or when maintenance is needed, such as removing damaged plant material.

### How long will my terrarium last?
A terrarium is a living arrangement, not a permanent or maintenance-free object. How long it lasts depends on suitable light, careful watering and occasional upkeep. With the right conditions, the plants and composition can be enjoyed for a long time, and the arrangement will change as the plants grow.

### What should I do if a leaf dies?
Remove dead or decaying leaves carefully with clean tools so they do not remain in the arrangement. If leaves keep dying, check the light, moisture and airflow conditions.
